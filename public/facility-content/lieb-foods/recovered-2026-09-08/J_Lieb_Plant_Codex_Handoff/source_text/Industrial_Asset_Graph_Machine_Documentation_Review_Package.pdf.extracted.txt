1. Project Summary
We are building one central equipment-knowledge system for the facility. It will hold machine records, electrical information,
controls, utilities, photographs, drawings, procedures, repair history, and the practical knowledge learned by experienced 
maintenance personnel.
The first machine is being documented completely so it can become the repeatable pattern for every other machine. The 
system must remain editable because field corrections, machine changes, replacements, and additions are inevitable.
What this project is meant to prevent
 Losing years of practical knowledge when an experienced electrician retires.
 Wasting time tracing the same wires, identifying the same parts, and rediscovering the same faults.
 Treating guesses, old drawings, or undocumented changes as verified facts.
 Keeping important information scattered across binders, phones, computers, and individual memory.
What the finished system should answer
 What panel and circuit feed this machine?
 What shuts this motor down?
 Which PLC input receives this sensor?
 What devices are in the safety circuit?
 Where are the disconnect and isolation points?
 What usually causes this alarm or failure?
 What part was used previously, and what was changed?
 Which information is confirmed and which still needs field verification?
2. Instructions for the Electrician Reviewing This
Please review this as a practical maintenance record, not as an engineering report. Mark up anything that is wrong, 
incomplete, or misleading.
☐ Correct machine names, component names, and functions.
☐ Cross out anything that is only a guess or is not true.
☐ Add the first things you normally check when the machine fails.
☐ Add recurring problems, odd behavior, and known weak points.
☐ Add startup, shutdown, reset, and recovery steps that are not in the manual.
☐ Identify important parts that should be kept on the shelf.
☐ Point out any modifications that do not match the original drawings.
☐ Mark anything that still needs to be traced, tested, photographed, or confirmed.
Most valuable information: the practical details that an experienced electrician knows but the OEM manual does not 
explain.
3. Evidence and Verification Rules
Every important fact should show how certain it is. Unknown information stays unknown until somebody verifies it.

5. Current Machine Summary
Purpose
The machine appears to form corrugated cartons from flat blanks. It uses powered motion, servo-controlled assemblies, 
pneumatics, vacuum, and adhesive-related functions. The exact production sequence must be reviewed with the electrician
and operators.
Working operating sequence - Field Verify
 Flat corrugated blanks are loaded into the magazine.
 A blank is picked or pulled into the forming area.
 The blank is opened and positioned around the forming section or mandrel.
 Flaps are folded in the programmed sequence.
 Adhesive is applied where required.
 The joint or flaps are pressed long enough to hold.
 The finished carton is discharged to the next section.
Observed major control equipment
 One Allen-Bradley PowerFlex 70 variable-frequency drive, labeled VFD1.
 Three Control Techniques servo drives. Exact model numbers and axis assignments require close-up label 
photographs and field confirmation.

6. Information Still Required
Electrical and controls
☐ Incoming disconnect manufacturer, model, rating, and upstream source.
☐ Exact panel, MCC, transformer, breaker, and circuit feeding the machine.
☐ PLC or controller manufacturer, model, firmware, and program-backup location.
☐ HMI manufacturer, model, software version, password-control method, and backup location.
☐ Complete remote-I/O module list, node information, and I/O map.
☐ VFD catalog number, motor served, horsepower, wiring, and parameter backup.
☐ Each servo-drive model, motor served, encoder type, home method, and parameter backup.
☐ Safety relay or safety-controller information and a verified safety-circuit description.
☐ Network devices, addresses, communication protocols, and cable routing.
☐ Terminal-strip map and field-wire destinations.
☐ Any undocumented jumpers, bypasses, field modifications, or abandoned wiring.
Motors, sensors, pneumatics, and mechanical items
☐ Every motor and gearbox nameplate, location, function, rotation, and driven assembly.
☐ Every photoeye, proximity switch, limit switch, pressure switch, vacuum switch, and safety switch.
☐ Every solenoid valve, cylinder, regulator, vacuum device, and normal pressure setting.
☐ Air-isolation point and method for releasing stored pneumatic energy.
☐ Belts, chains, bearings, lubrication points, tension guidance, and common wear items.
☐ Glue-system manufacturer, model, operating settings, isolation points, and common faults.
Operation and maintenance knowledge
☐ Normal startup and shutdown procedure.
☐ Restart after power loss or emergency stop.
☐ Recipe list and product/changeover information.
☐ Normal speed and important operator settings.
☐ Top recurring failures and quickest checks.
☐ Alarm list with plain-language causes and recovery steps.
☐ Preventive-maintenance schedule based on actual plant conditions.
☐ Critical spare parts and known substitute parts.
☐ Repair and modification history.
☐ Anything the retiring electrician considers important but undocumented.
7. Industrial Asset Graph Repository Integration
This machine package will become part of the existing Industrial Asset Graph repository. The repository is the facility's 
long-term industrial knowledge base, and the graph is one way to view equipment and its relationships.
Basic asset hierarchy
Facility -> Building -> Area -> Production Line -> Machine -> Assembly -> Panel or Component

Assets must also support relationships outside that hierarchy, such as: panel feeds VFD, VFD controls motor, sensor 
reports to PLC input, safety relay removes enable power, compressor supplies machine air, and upstream equipment 
enables downstream equipment.
Required machine package
 A human-readable document for electricians, mechanics, supervisors, contractors, training, and printed use.
 A machine-readable record for search, filtering, dependency tracing, reports, AI assistance, and future CMMS 
integration.
 Evidence files such as photographs, manuals, drawings, PLC backups, and VFD parameters.
 Relationships connecting the machine to panels, utilities, controls, components, and other equipment.
 A verification checklist separating confirmed information from assumptions.

Development rules
 Inspect the current repository before changing its architecture.
 Preserve working functions and map new data into existing schemas where possible.
 Keep machine data separate from application code.
 Extend schemas only when needed and validate required fields and broken relationships.
 Keep fictional or sample data clearly separated from verified facility data.
 Favor plain labels and practical navigation over unnecessary software complexity.
 Do not build the first machine as a one-off static screen.
