production when condensate is returned and water is lost through blowdown.

2. WHAT MUST BE MEASURED TO ANSWER THE STEAM QUESTION
Required measurement How to obtain it Why it matters
Makeup water Record boiler water-meter start/end readings at the same time each day. Establishes new water entering the boiler system.
Boiler runtime Record burner/boiler operating hours for the same measurement period. Converts daily totals into operating-hour rates.
Condensate return Use a condensate-return meter if installed. If none exists, measure/estimate tank inflow only by a documented method. Returned condensate can be reused and converted to steam repeatedly.
Blowdown Record continuous blowdown flow if metered. For manual blowdown, log every event, duration, and known/measured flow. Blowdown consumes boiler water without becoming useful plant steam.
Feedwater Record feedwater temperature at consistent operating conditions. Needed to track system condition and report's 180 F recommendation.
Chemical use Record tank/drum level or weight at start/end plus pump settings. Allows chemical consumption to be normalized to water and steam load.
Boiler load Record steam pressure and, if available, burner firing rate or steam-flow meter. Provides operating context; a steam-flow meter is the preferred direct measurement.

3. FIELD PROCEDURE - RUN THIS AUDIT FOR AT LEAST 7 NORMAL
PRODUCTION DAYS
1. Pick one consistent daily reading time. Use the same time every day.
2. Before changing chemical settings, document the current chemical products, pump names, stroke/speed/timer settings, drum
levels, and any controller setpoints.
3. Record the makeup-water meter reading.
4. Record boiler operating hours for the previous 24 hours.
5. Record feedwater temperature, boiler pressure, feedwater conductivity, boiler conductivity, and other treatment tests normally
used by the site.
6. Record every manual blowdown event. If there is continuous blowdown, determine its actual flow rate if safely measurable.
7. Record condensate return quantity if a meter exists. If no meter exists, mark it UNKNOWN rather than inventing a percentage.
8. Record chemical drum/tank usage. A marked sight level, scale/weight, calibrated tank volume, or verified pump delivery can be
used.
9. Note production shutdowns, sanitation/CIP, steam leaks, failed traps, unusual venting, boiler trips, water-softener regeneration,
or major load changes.
10. After the audit period, calculate averages. Compare chemical usage against BOTH makeup water and estimated/measured
steam.

6. CALCULATIONS
A. Makeup water used (gal) = Ending water-meter reading - Starting water-meter reading
B. Makeup water rate (gal/day) = Makeup gallons / number of days
C. Makeup water mass (lb/day) = Makeup gal/day x 8.34 lb/gal (approximate water density for field estimating)
D. Blowdown gallons = Blowdown flow (gal/min) x total blowdown minutes
E. Condensate return % - calculate only when total condensate returned and total steam/condensate basis are actually
measured on a consistent basis. Do not back-calculate a 'precise' percentage from makeup water alone.
F. Steam production - BEST METHOD: use a calibrated steam-flow meter and totalize lb/day or lb/hr. If no steam meter exists,
steam must be estimated from a defensible mass balance using measured makeup, condensate return, blowdown, and other
